Wind Alert · web extension pitch

Forecast & history page — four directions

Same data underneath (4 providers, aggregated, plus what actually happened), four very different ways to live with it. All fake-but-plausible data for Sopot. Pick the one we build.

Concept A

The Board — dense hourly grid

Windguru raised you; this respects that. One screen, every hour, every number. History is just yesterday's column set — with the observed row underneath so you see who lied. Reads like an instrument panel, not a website.

Feels like Windguru with taste. Zero learning curve for any surfer.
Strong at scanning a whole day in two seconds; comparing days; the observed-vs-forecast row is brutal and honest.
Weak at phones (it scrolls sideways — acceptable, but it's a desktop soul); telling you when to go — you still read it yourself.
Build cost lowest. One templated table, htmx day-paging, no JS charting.
Concept B

Sessions — the answer, not the data

Phone-first. The engine already knows your rules — so the page speaks in sessions, not hours: when to go, how big, how sure. History becomes a season strip — every day you could have ridden. The forecast table still exists, one tap deeper.

Feels like a knowledgeable friend texting you. The only concept that works one-handed on a beach parking lot.
Strong at the actual decision (“go at 13:00, take the 9 m²”); making the rule engine visible and trustworthy; safety badges (offshore) impossible to miss.
Weak at free exploration — if the rules are badly tuned, the page is only as smart as they are; power users will want the raw numbers anyway.
Build cost medium. Needs the session-window logic (improvement #2) done first; cards + strip are simple templates after that.
Concept C

The Explorer — one continuous timeline

Past and future are one river. Observed wind flows in from the left as solid ink; the provider envelope opens to the right of now. Your rule is a quiet band on the wall — the moment the river enters the band, that's your session. Hover anywhere, get every provider's word. Drag the minimap to sail weeks back.

Feels like a proper instrument — the one screen that explains every alert you ever got.
Strong at provider disagreement (the band is the confidence); forecast-vs-reality at a glance; “why did/didn't it fire” debugging; pure pleasure to pan around.
Weak at instant answers — it rewards looking, not glancing; the most JS of the three (one hand-rolled SVG chart, ~300 lines, still no library).
Build cost highest. Needs forecast + observation storage (improvements #16/#19) to shine; chart itself is self-contained vanilla JS.
Concept D

The Window — living map over a scrubbable timeline

Windy-style, and the marriage is one shared clock. The map above shows one moment — animated particles carry strength (color, length, pace) and direction, engine borrowed from ../wind-range-widget. The timeline below shows all moments — the Explorer chart, demoted to a scrubber but keeping envelope, rule band and observed line. Drag the cursor: the sky obeys. Press ▶ and watch the weekend happen.

Feels like Windy, but it's your spot, your rule, and reality drawn next to the forecast.
Strong at physical intuition — direction and strength hit before you read a single number; one time-cursor marries glance (map) and depth (chart) with zero mode switching; play = the whole weekend in ten seconds.
Weak at comparing hours side by side (the map shows one moment — the Board shows thirty); always-dark panel inside a light page is a taste call; needs a coastline silhouette per location (vendored asset — no CDN tiles).
Build cost highest of the four. Particle engine exists in ../wind-range-widget (vendor ~150 lines, swap sweep-cycle for scrub-driven config); scrubber is Concept C's chart + pointer drag; same storage needs as C (#16/#19).
How to choose: Board if the page is for reading conditions, Sessions if it's for deciding, Explorer if it's for understanding, Window if it's for feeling it before you drive. Window is itself a marriage — glanceable widget on top, deep-dive timeline below, one clock. They also stack — Sessions on top, Explorer one tap deeper — but we start with one.
